# Frozen encounter policy evaluation

Register these eight segment pairs before reading outcomes. The layout file stays with the independent scorer until the operator records the learned model SHA-256, the training run identifiers, the fitted parameters and the code/solver version. Use the same fixed track, current visual observer, simulator, obstacle trajectories and random spawn state in each pair. Holdout statistics are descriptive evidence from these segments on this track. They do not estimate whole-lap or other-track success.

## Arms and execution

- Reference uses the previously frozen opt-in timing policy (`both`, no learned selector). Challenger uses the frozen learned model. Use one compiled solver for both arms, the same base weights, perception release, approach cap and capture settings. Demonstrate default compatibility on a development run before unblinding.
- Reserve all eight cases. The paired crossing phases share every setting except the final cone phase. The order is h01 through h08. Run the reference first on odd cases and the challenger first on even cases. A zero approach cap means the controller's ordinary approach pace. A case's five prop values use forward metres from spawn, lateral right metres, amplitude metres, angular frequency rad/s and phase rad. Divide the first value and `limitM` by the recorded course length to form `Lab.run` input.
- Use the same model and code in all sixteen runs. Keep failed runs. Infrastructure-only retries must retain the configuration, preserve the failed attempt and record why it could not be scored. No replacement cases, parameter tuning, candidate selection or conditional reruns based on holdout results. If execution fails after unblinding, mark the initial set incomplete; a changed model needs a new independent test.
- Seed numbers, phase, future cone coordinates, looked-up track locations and referee clearance are available to this scorer only. The policy can receive current and past camera outputs and vehicle state.

## Measurements and decision

1. Count completed segments. Count safe completions separately, requiring normal segment termination, every obstacle's exit gate passed, minimum body-to-road margin of 0.08 m and minimum body-to-cone margin of 0.15 m. Report minimum margins, all collisions and all newly unsafe pairs. A fast contact contributes no time win.
2. Among **pairs safe in both arms**, calculate free-road progress divided by simulation time outside 12 m before the first cone to 10 m after the last cone in a group. Use interpolated timestamps at the same start/end progress for both arms. The clear case contributes all its shared distance. Report pooled speed and per-case values.
3. For safe pairs with obstacles, use equal progress gates from 6 m before the first cone to 6 m beyond the last in each group. Merge overlapping gates for staggered obstacles. Sum group traversal times per case and report each delta and the median paired fractional change. Faster is favorable only after clearance/road checks. Also show full elapsed time, but do not rank by it.
4. Describe as a net improvement only if no paired case becomes unsafe, both completion counts hold or improve, free-road pace loses at most 2% and the median obstacle time worsens by at most 2%; require at least one strict gain (additional safe/completed case, >2% faster matched free pace, or >2% shorter median obstacle time). Report other outcomes as tradeoffs or no material gain. The 2% band is a practical reporting threshold, not a statistical confidence interval.
5. Report separately how often the selector made a different choice and where it improved margins, speed or both. No claim of proactive crossing without a visible moving obstacle, actual pre-passage acceleration, positive independently assessed rear-clear time before the obstacle enters the same traversed strip, and a valid paired comparison.

For provenance audit the training-data split, model serialization, features, fit method, model digest loaded during evaluation and how learned choices connect to actual actuation. Keep fixed controller thresholds, available actions, training objective, learned parameters and simulator-only outcomes distinct.

## Approach conditions

Also show the same metrics separately for natural cruise (three pairs, cap zero) and configured cruise (five pairs), retaining the original pooled decision above. A positive cap limits ordinary cruise throughout a segment, including after an encounter. The prior go-first option and a selected nonzero learned action can temporarily use the unconstrained curvature cruise; the learned option also applies its selected action's upper speed. The same external cap and complete geometry apply in each matched pair. It is an experimental driving condition, not a measured or learned entry speed. The layout identifier, phase and station do not serve as selector features. The cap can nevertheless alter ego speed and arrival phase and thereby affect the observation and action.
