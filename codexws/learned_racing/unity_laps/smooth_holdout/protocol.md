# Frozen assessment of smoother obstacle driving and 4 m/s cruising

## Study design

The primary reference is the previously trained V2 action selector (file SHA-256 `22d6f47258e079bd74a93574010c02f78afb605e38c7fe8191d23a93cc4ea729`). The challenger is one frozen selector chosen on development/validation runs. Both use identical perception, simulator vehicle, physics, spawn, layout, ordinary cruise cap, observation timing and compiled control implementation. First reproduce the incumbent on a previously seen case with the new solver. The speed of 4 m/s is a **measured cruise target**, distinct from a wheel or velocity command.

Reserve every case from training, model selection and validation. Save training/validation case IDs, deployment model and solver hashes, checkpoint selection rule and candidate validation results before revealing the layouts. One seeded pair is the observational unit; telemetry frames are repeated measurements. No per-case tuning, selective replacement, or removal of crashes. Log infrastructure retries and preserve attempts. A changed model needs another independent assessment.

- `b01–b24`: four clear straight controls, four single-static, four staggered-static, four crossing, four central-moving and four adjacent mixed encounters. Encounter groups cover both bends and straights. Nineteen local trials use the natural cruise setting and five have explicit caps for both arms.
- `l01–l08`: six natural full laps with fresh static/compound/moving layouts and two natural full laps with no added obstacles, all with unique random spawns and empty controller state. Terminal gate 32 is required for a lap. These evaluate the current track only. Incidental scene objects and perception errors remain in all cases.
- Run base then challenger in odd-numbered cases, challenger then base in even cases, retaining all 64 runs. Store nonselected checkpoints and optional v2 diagnoses outside the holdout set. Prior whole laps do not enter the fresh denominators.

## Physical safety and progress

Score each segment and lap as successful only when Unity reports normal completion, the expected gates were reached, minimum body-to-cone separation is positive, and minimum body-to-road margin is nonnegative. Report collisions, boundary departures, stalled or lost-lane runs, other stops, elapsed time, matched progress, terminal location and distance still missing. Compare discordant completion counts and identify every new failure. No positive 0.5 m, 0.15 m or 0.08 m threshold is imposed. For all approached cones and observed road minima report the raw signed margins as well as counts in negative/contact, (0,.10), [.10,.25), [.25,.50), [.50,1.0) and ≥1.0 m bands; list unvisited obstacles separately. A road margin exactly zero counts at the near-zero boundary according to Unity's own `road < 0` termination rule.

## Measuring steering and speed together

The obstacle window runs from 12 m before its fixed longitudinal station to 8 m after. Merge overlapping windows; this prevents counting a close pair twice. Use actual run prop stations equally in both arms. Clip signals to the same progress gates by interpolating timestamps. For each window report path length measured in the XZ plane, excess path length versus longitudinal progress (can be negative on an inner turn), occupied time, progress speed, integrated absolute **measured** yaw rate, integrated signed yaw, excess heading travel (absolute minus net), yaw-rate total variation, and sign reversals crossing a ±0.08 rad/s deadband. Express yaw travel/variation and reversals per 10 metres as well as raw values. Also report the absolute right-minus-left commanded wheel target in RPM (time mean) and its sampled total variation. This vehicle is differential drive. Wheel command variation and measured body rotation describe different things.

Primary geometry-matched smoothness is the median paired fractional change in integrated |yaw rate| per 10 m of the common obstacle window, one weighted-by-distance window aggregate per case. Check the pooled difference, excess heading travel, reversal counts, yaw-rate variation, differential RPM, and excess path as supporting evidence. Straight and bend groups are shown separately. A near-zero reference (<0.001 rad/10 m) gives no stable percentage; still show both raw values. A changing path around a cone can require genuine turning.

Compare obstacle speed/time over identical gates in physical-success pairs; incomplete cases receive **no time or smoothness win**. Also compute diagnostic common observed-prefix metrics for every pair (including failures), with window coverage/distance and unvisited windows. Publish all success→failure, failure→success and mutually failed cases; do not infer stability from a shorter collision-truncated trace. Report all-segment/lap completion first, and successful full lap times only when both arms completed. Separately show time differences for free road and the same obstacle gates.

An overall smoother-and-no-slower claim requires no new physical failure in **either** cohort, no decrease in completion in either, at least 5% reduction in local median paired yaw travel per 10 m, no more than 2% longer median matched local obstacle traversal time, and no more than 2% slower pooled matched natural straight cruise. If any key comparison lacks jointly completed cases, the claim is unverified. Speed target attainment and individual tradeoffs are reported even if this combined gate fails. The percentages are practical thresholds for this simulator study.

## Natural straight cruise

`straightCruiseGatesM` in the sealed matrix lists all primary cruise windows. For b01–b04 the gate is exactly 12–34 m after spawn. The four different spawns were selected using track shape only so the complete gate is straight. For other natural cases identify contiguous 0.25 m cells with local absolute centreline curvature ≤0.012/m after the first 12 m and outside all added-obstacle approach/pass windows; trim each end by 2 m, retain original intervals of length ≥12 m before trimming. Report clear local, clear full lap, and traffic full-lap cruise separately. Explicit cap cases, bends, starting transients and added-obstacle windows are disclosed as separate conditions; their slow speeds are not discarded to manufacture a clear-road result.

For every predeclared gate report observed progress / actual elapsed time (m/s), the fraction of the **planned** distance the reported physical forward body speed was ≥4.00 m/s, and the fraction of planned distance actually observed. Interpolate physical speed at 10 Hz; never substitute target speed. A slow observation contributes to the denominator. An unreached distance earns zero ≥4 m/s metres and stays visible; elapsed time on unknown distance cannot be imputed. Mark missing/partial gates and compute paired speed only over shared observed progress. Report the longest continuous ≥4 m/s distance within one gate to distinguish sustained cruise from spikes. Track-wide average and the free-road speed reported in previous revisions are different measures.

Declare the 4 m/s natural-clear target demonstrated only when **all four** clear local segments and **both** clear laps complete, each local fixed gate's mean is ≥4.00 m/s and each clear lap's pooled fixed straight-gate mean is ≥4.00 m/s. Report high-speed distance percentage even if this stringent test fails; it has no separately invented threshold. For traffic laps, report geometry-only predeclared straight coverage regardless of success and any ≥4 m/s attainment there separately.

## Paired uncertainty

Show each pair, effect signs and the counts of better/tied/worse outcomes. Report per-cohort paired bootstrap 95% intervals (10,000 seeded resamples; resample complete case pairs with replacement, never individual frames) for completion-rate difference, median yaw/time changes, and pooled cruise differences where comparable. Each sample reflects this deliberately mixed finite test on one simulated course, not an estimate of independent tracks. Very small strata and all-identical effects have limited uncertainty information.
