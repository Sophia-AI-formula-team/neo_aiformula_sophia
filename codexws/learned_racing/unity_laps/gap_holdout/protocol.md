# Independent two-lane opening assessment

Seal all eight new seeded local layouts and geometry witnesses before receiving a trained gap planner. Every gate places a whole cone in each traffic lane over its entire motion. There are two static, two moving, two staggered double and two mixed triple gate layouts. Five layouts contain curved gates. Positions, gate indices, phases, witnessing path, and curve shape are referee-only inputs. Record the frozen previous smooth policy, new planner and cruise multiplier before revealing any layouts. The primary pair is the same previous model, production perception, new compiled solver, settings, and seeded physics with only the gap configuration enabled on the learned arm. Add an optional pace-only arm for every layout using the same free speed increase with the opening planner disabled. Keep these tests separate from the original 32 pairs, training and development. All approaches use natural cruise; no imposed entrance cap. Alternate the first arm between layouts.

Each layout has a precomputed geometry-only path running from spawn through all its gate midpoints. Reconstruct Unity's outer-lane centre, road normals, discrete placement and approximate road margin. Use the 0.76 m wide reference rectangle (0.28 m forward, 0.88 m rearward) and 0.34 m radius cones. Check all phases by bounding each cone's entire continuous swept centre line segment in the current vehicle axes, then subtract its radius. Check road clearance with all four corners. Record a margin reserve larger than twice the maximum adjacent corner displacement; path samples are spaced up to 0.015 m apart. This certifies open physical space through the sequence. These reference paths have not been driven by a motor controller and do not certify feasibility at an arbitrary speed.

Primary binary outcomes are (1) physical completion, Unity segment, positive minimum cone margins, nonnegative road margin, all props exited, and (2) physical completion **and every gate taken through the middle**. Count them out of all eight (also score each gate out of all 14). A middle passage requires the whole oriented footprint to lie between the true moving cone edges throughout front-entry to rear-exit of the common gate slab (cone radius on both sides). Interpolate logged body pose and unwrapped heading at 5 ms; use exact sinusoid at the corresponding time. For each side require projected separation >0.01 m, reserving 1 cm for a route label, independent of the official physical contact threshold of 0. Clear outside passages, ambiguous straddles and unreached gates are separately labelled. Record nominal outer-lane-line-open or lateral-shift-required, the distances and times to each failed gate, start/end body speeds, lateral total travel, signed displacement, and min left/right projected space, axle offset, actual cone and road minima. Referee gap over the car's entire rectangle and full run minimum physical separation answer different questions.

Speed is the main ranking **among physical successes**. Report paired fixed approach-to-exit times (-8 m from the first gate to +7 m from the last) and net progress speed only on common successes, with per-gate front/rear body speed and group elapsed time. Also show the subset when both completed all middle passages. No shortened crash path earns a pace win. Show per-case terminal status/progress, times saved/lost, wins/ties/losses, paired uncertainty (whole cases, not individual gates), and the same comparison against the pace-only arm if present. A demonstrated increase in middle passage requires at least one new all-middle completion and no case losing physical success; a general speed improvement additionally requires lower median matched common-window time, without an all-middle or physical regression. This small fixed one-track study establishes these conditions only. Do not interpret a single successful film as universal performance.
